## 3. Taxonomy

Two tiers, standard and specialty, at two levels. The words are the screen's, Standard and Specialty as Settings' Units says them, and the code says the same: `tier: 'standard' | 'specialty'` on every unit and category, and `specialty` as the Specialty section's key, renamed from `basic`, `advanced`, and `specialist` on 2026-10-16 so code, docs, and screen share one vocabulary, saved settings read over (Sean, 2026-10-16). Keys stay apart from labels (the web's `SECTION_LABEL`), so a later change of word is a label's, not a stored key's. A category's `group` (common, mechanics, digital, and the rest below) is its field, for grouping categories (docs/PLAN.md §2), not a tier.

- **Category tier.** Every category the engine has is listed on the start and in the category menu (ADR 0016). The specialty groups below are planned for loading on demand (milestone 3); how a long list of categories is divided is decided when they arrive.
- **Unit tier.** Every unit is listed in its category's table, standard and specialty alike (ADR 0016). The tier places it: standard units are listed by system, specialty ones after them all, each group smallest to largest (ADR 0011); and among equal matches, or exact ones, standard comes first. A unit in everyday use in one region is standard (the UK fluid ounce, pint, and gallon; the US short ton). The tier is kept with care on every unit: Settings' Specialty switch shows or hides the specialty ones (ADR 0018). A specialty unit is one someone converts for a trade, a hobby, or the news; retired units, units that depend on conditions, a second name for a unit already listed (an alias instead), and curiosities stay out.

## 5. Aliases and disambiguation

Each unit lists the aliases people actually type: symbols with and without case (`ml`, `mL`), spellings (`litre`, `liter`), plurals, abbreviations (`tbsp`, `tbs`), and regional names. Category aliases cover the words people use (`distance`, `how far`, `fluids`, `liquid`, `temp`, `hot`, `storage`).

An alias is added only when people really type it to mean that unit. It counts as an exact match, so it outranks every prefix match in other categories (`kilo` puts kilogram above kilometer, docs/UX-SPEC.md §4). An alias that makes a symbol ambiguous is deliberate (`oz` on fluid ounce), and the list then shows each unit with its category.

Ambiguity watchlist (exact matches across categories or variants): `c` (Celsius's alias, the speed of light's symbol; the standard unit leads), `nm` and `Nm` (the nanometer's symbol, the newton meter's alias) and `kn` and `kN` (the knot's symbol, the kilonewton's): the current category's leads, then a key written as typed, its case included, so each spelling names its own unit (Sean, 2026-10-16: `500 nm` is nanometers), `ft-lb` (Torque's pound-foot and Energy's foot-pound), `m` and `w` (the meter's and the watt's symbols; the US scales' shorthand keys count only where the category is named), `oz`, `ton`, `gal`, `pt`, `qt`, `cup`, `cal`, `hp`, `mpg`, `kB`, `lb` (mass vs force), `mi`, `min`, `s`, `gr` (the grain's symbol, which some write for grams), `degrees` (the Temperature category's word and the angle's unit), `how long` (an alias of Length and of Time, so both category rows show, Length first by registry order), and `′` `″` (arcminute and arcsecond, which planned feet-and-inches entry may write for feet and inches). Nothing commits until Enter or a pick, and for these the list shows every candidate with its category, so the choice is visible before committing.

No-match queries are the best signal for missing aliases. Log them (locally in dev, via analytics later) and review before each release.

## 6. Adding a category or unit

1. Create or open `packages/core/src/units/<category>.ts`. Write units most common first: lists order them by tier, by the visitor's system, and by size (ADR 0011, ADR 0016), and this order breaks ties and orders a category with no size (temperature).
2. Add the unit with `id`, `category`, `name`, `plural`, `symbol`, `aliases`, `system`, `tier`, its `kind` with a `factor` (plus an `offset` when affine; divided by the value when reciprocal) or a function pair, `exact`, `source`, and a `note` when its definition has a variant.
3. Check the factor on our own against the standard that defines it, wherever it was found (§2, ADR 0020), and add at least one golden row from that standard in `packages/core/test/golden/<category>.ts`.
4. Add the unit to this catalog under the right tier.
5. If the unit introduces a definition choice (like cup), add it to the special-logic table.
6. **An ingredient** (Cooking, ADR 0021) goes in `packages/core/src/units/ingredients.ts` with its id, name, kind (the heading it is listed under, `INGREDIENT_KINDS`, in whose order the data runs), aliases, its cup's weight filled as US recipes mean it, that method, the weighings that count with their figures (`sources`) and their spread, and a note where another method matters. Its figure is docs/research/cooking-2.md's rule, the general method's for a measured fact (docs/RESEARCH.md §2, ADR 0022; the `research` skill): screen every source found, read each that would count, add it to `docs/research/cooking-2/figures.py`, and regenerate (`write_ingredients.py`, then Prettier, and `write_doc.py`); core's test recomputes the figure from its sources (`cupFigure`), a liquid's within its density's references. A unit of its own (a stick) goes in `src/units/cooking.ts` with `ingredient`, and a golden row.
7. A new category also needs its id in `CategoryId` (`src/types.ts`), its file registered in `src/units/index.ts`, its golden file listed in `test/golden/index.ts`, and its id in the category lists of `test/registry.test.ts` and `test/search.test.ts`, all under `packages/core`.
8. **A pair** (ADR 0026) is a unit of its category with `pair`, the ids of its parts largest first, its `factor` the first part's, its `symbol` the parts' symbols with a space (`ft in`), its name and plural the quantity's (`feet, inches`), its source the standard that fixes the parts' ratio, a `notation` where it is written other than by its symbols (marks attached, `['′', '″']`, or `'clock'`), and a golden row of one of it in its last part (1 ft in = 12 in); its id in `test/registry.test.ts`'s list of pairs. The reader needs nothing more for a pair whose parts are named by their keys; a spelling that names a part only in that pair (`m` for minutes after an hour) goes in `compound.ts`'s context table, and a mark in its marks table.
9. **A scale** (ADR 0027): a unit marked in steps carries `step` (the half size, 5 mm), and the web's rows write a converted value to it, ≈ where that moved it; one written before its number carries `prefix`; one agreed by a body rather than defined carries `conventional` and `exact: false`; one defined by a table rather than a formula is `functional`, straight lines between the table's rows, with `range`, the span of base values it covers. The table is typed once, in the research folder's data file (`docs/research/shoe-sizes-1/tables.py`), and a generator writes core's copies (`write_tables.py`); the test holds the engine to the standard's own table, every cell, naming the misses the standard's rounding explains. A category whose figures need a word under the table carries `note`.
10. **A brand's shoe-size chart** (ADR 0028, ADR 0029) is data core is given, `packages/core/data/shoe-brands.json`, written by `docs/research/shoe-sizes-2/write_brands.py` from the record `brands.py` writes (`brands.json`), never by hand, and fetched by the web app once Shoe size opens: a new brand's chart goes through the research method into the record (a worker's `charts.csv` or the manual capture, checked), `brands.py` is re-run, then `write_brands.py` and Prettier; its name, aliases, shorthand keys (which count only where the category is named), site, and the fact the note carries are typed once in `src/units/shoe-brands.ts`, and `test/shoe-brands.test.ts` holds core to the record cell for cell.
