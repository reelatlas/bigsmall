# UX specification — the converter

Current behavior of the build, 2026-10-15: the converter, one box and its table (ADR 0012, ADR 0013), and the About page. This document describes what the converter does now; anything not built yet is under **Planned** (§14). Change this document before changing behavior, in the same change. How each rule got here, pass by pass, is recorded in `docs/research/findings-0-first-pass.md`.

- **The wordmark at play.** While a mouse is over the heading (the name and its art, not the menus), the site name turns into something else and back, like a split-flap board: each letter flips through symbols and lands, left to right. Twelve kinds of phrase, each drawn by a weight the lab tunes: "big" or "small" replaced by a unit, number, or symbol (`∞small`, `bigµm`), both replaced (`TBnm`), letters swapped for look-alikes or capitals (`bιgSmall`), opposite pairs (`giganano`, `km:mm`), analogies of size (`whale:krill`, `oak:acorn`, `Everest:anthill`), idioms of size and measure (`drop in the ocean`, `a stone’s throw`, `byte-sized`), equations (`E = mc²`, `Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2`), constants (`α ≈ 1/137`, `c = 299 792 458 m/s`), conversions (`−40 °C = −40 °F`), wordplay on the name (`small ⊂ big`, `∫small = big`), and text art (`▁▂▃▄▅▆▇`): over a thousand phrases, never the same twice in a row. The first change comes a quarter of a second after the pointer arrives, and at rest it then changes every 2 s; moving the pointer speeds it up at once, the faster the movement the faster it goes, down to about every 0.1 s, then eases back as the pointer slows. Each phrase takes the largest type size at which it fits the name's width, from the name's own size down to `sm`; from `base` down, a long phrase takes two lines before a smaller size ("big fish," over "small pond"), broken at a space or after a colon and never inside a grouped number. A flipping letter is never wider than the room its phrase leaves, so no frame runs past the name, and nothing is clipped, so descenders and tall symbols show whole; nothing beside the name moves. The art plays along: from its drawing it flips to a symbol or a short expression in the theme's accent (`π`, `ℏ`, `c²`) with a little under half the name's changes, landing after the name's letters, and back to its drawing with about half, so it shows a symbol about half the time. Leaving the heading lets the name and the art land back home. On a touch screen, a tap on the heading plays it for four seconds. The heading's name stays "bigsmall" for screen readers and search engines throughout; under reduced motion nothing plays. Its code (`lib/wordmark.ts`, `lib/wordmark-motion.ts`) runs only once the heading is hovered or tapped, and every setting is tuned on `/dev/wordmark-lab` (docs/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md, "Tuning labs").

## 2. Parsing

The box's text is parsed on every keystroke into `{ raw, value?, valueText?, query?, outOfRange?, invalid?, expression?, exponentMarks? }` (`packages/core/src/parse.ts`): a leading number or arithmetic, if any, and the rest as a unit query in its original case.

**Number grammar.** Optional sign (`-`, `+`, or the Unicode minus), digit groups separated by `.`, `,`, a non-breaking space, or a narrow non-breaking space, an optional fraction, an optional exponent (`1e6`, `2.5E-3`), and the leading-separator forms `.5` and `,5`. Which mark is the decimal:

- two different marks present: the last one is the decimal (`1,234.5` and `1.234,5` both work);
- one mark used more than once: grouping (`1,000,000`);
- one mark used once: the locale's reading, unless the tail is not exactly three digits, which can only be a fraction (`1,5` is 1.5 even in en-US; `1,000` is a thousand in en-US and one in de-DE).

**Times.** A time written as a clock writes it is a number too, each part sixtieths of the one before: `5:30` is 5 and 30 sixtieths of the unit after it, so `5:30 /km`, `5:30/km`, and `5:30 min/km` are a pace of five minutes thirty seconds a kilometer, and `1:30 h` is an hour and a half; `1:05:30` has three parts. Every part after the first is two digits under 60: `5:` and `5:3` wait for the next key, as a decimal mark does, and `5:75` is no time. Alone, `5:30` asks for a unit, as a number does (Sean, 2026-10-16).

Non-breaking spaces always group, and so does an apostrophe before a group of exactly three digits, as Swiss numbers are written (`1'234'567`), while `5'10` keeps its apostrophe for the unit. Digits in any script read as their values (`١٢٣`, `१२३`), as do fullwidth forms (`１２．５`), the Arabic decimal and group marks, and a locale's exponent written in letters; the marks Intl sets around a minus for a script's direction are passed over. So the box reads back every number the table writes, in any locale (the accuracy audit, 2026-10-15). Whitespace between the number and the unit is optional.

A decimal mark still waiting for its digits belongs to the number (`5.` on the way to `5.5` is 5).

**Arithmetic.** The number can be arithmetic: numbers joined by `+ − × ÷ ^` (or `-`, `*`, `/`), with signs and parentheses, spacing optional (`2 × 3 ft` is 6 and `ft`, `2^10 bytes` is 1024 bytes). `^` comes first and groups from the right (`2^3^2` is 2^9), and before a sign, so `−2^2` is −4 and `2^−3` is 0.125; then `×` and `÷`; then `+` and `−`. It ends where something else begins, so `5 km/h` is still 5 and `km/h`: an operator joins the arithmetic only before another number, a sign, or `(`, or at the very end. Unfinished arithmetic is read as far as it goes while typing: a trailing operator or `(` is ignored and open parentheses close at the end (`5 +` is 5, `(5 + 3` is 8). `expression` is set once an operation was evaluated. A small hand-written parser in core does it, never `eval`. Not yet: `x` as times, implied multiplication (`2(3)`).

**Out of range.** A number a double cannot hold is still a number: `1e400` sets `outOfRange: 'large'`, and nonzero digits that round to zero (`1e-400`) set `'small'`; arithmetic that overflows or underflows does the same; `value` is absent in both cases. Dividing by zero, including `0^−1`, sets `invalid: 'divide-by-zero'`, and a power with no real value (`(−8)^0.5`) sets `invalid: 'not-real'`, both without a value.

**Query.** Whatever follows the number, trimmed, in its original case. Search folds it (§4); the parser does not.

| -------------------- | ------ | -------- |
| `5`                  | 5      | —        |
| `5 ml`               | 5      | `ml`     |
| `5ml`                | 5      | `ml`     |
| `ml`                 | —      | `ml`     |
| `-40 f`              | -40    | `f`      |
| `1,000.5 km` (en-US) | 1000.5 | `km`     |
| `1.000,5 km` (de-DE) | 1000.5 | `km`     |
| `fluids`             | —      | `fluids` |

## 3. Listing units

**Listed order.** A category's units are listed the visitor's way (ADR 0011, ADR 0016, ADR 0018), in sections by system under fixed names, the same in every category: on a fresh visit the visitor's own system first (US (customary) for US visitors, UK (imperial) for UK visitors, Metric for everyone else, from the browser's region), then the other main systems, then Other (the knot and the atmosphere, standard units no region owns), then a category's paces in a Pace section of their own (Speed's min/km and min/mi, the visitor's own first; they read the other way, a smaller pace faster, in minutes and seconds; Sean, 2026-10-16), then Specialty; the visitor can reorder them in Units (§1), Other with the rest (Sean, 2026-10-15; it followed the last of Metric, US, and UK until then). Specialty is the specialty units of every system (Sean, 2026-10-14: the name pairs with Standard, and stands until a better one comes; the code has said the same since 2026-10-16); a category with no regional system (time, data, angle) lists its standard units under Standard. Only headings take the parentheses; units keep their names and symbols. The shared inch, foot, and pound sit with US (customary), or with UK (imperial) for a UK visitor. Within a section, units run smallest to largest; units of one size keep the catalog's order, most common first. So a US visitor's lengths read in, ft, yd, mi, then mm, cm, m, km, then, under Specialty, nm, µm, mil, fathom, furlong, nautical mile, au, light-year, parsec. Where size cannot order a category, because a unit has an offset (temperature) or is a reciprocal (fuel economy), the catalog's order stands with the visitor's own standard unit first: °F, °C, K in the US, °C, °F, K elsewhere; mpg (US) first in the US, mpg (UK) in the UK, L/100 km elsewhere. The table lists its rows in this order, and it never depends on the value.

**A temperature as a change** (Sean, 2026-10-16, the lab's D). Temperature converts a reading, through the scales' zero points; after the readings, a section says the box's number as a change too, a difference such as a forecast's or an oven's: every scale shown but the box's own, by the scales' degree sizes alone, so 5 °C warmer is 9 °F warmer, where a reading of 5 °C is 41 °F. Its heading says the amount: "As a change: 5 °C warmer or cooler"; a negative amount says its direction itself, "As a change: −25 °F", and every row keeps the sign typed, no "+" added. With no number yet, it reads "As a change" over dashes. It always shows with Temperature, with no switch (docs/PLAN.md §2, "Sections within a category"); Edit lists the units, never their change again; a change row copies as it shows, and taking it brings its scale with that number, the same change said in it.

**What shows** (ADR 0018) has two layers. The system switches in Settings (Metric, US (customary), UK (imperial), Other, Specialty) set every category; Standard always shows, since it is the whole standard list of a category with no regional system (Sean, 2026-10-15: Other always showed until then, with no switch); so does Pace, standard and with no switch yet (how a category's own sections are switched is a later exploration, docs/PLAN.md §2). Your region shows the visitor's own system and Other. A unit hidden or shown on its own in a table is an exception to its switch, kept when the category or another system's switch changes. Its own system's switch is final: unchecking Specialty hides every specialty unit, one shown on its own too, and checking it shows them all; a preset sets exactly its set, every exception cleared; Show custom hidden units, or the table itself, clears the hidden ones. While a system's units are chosen on their own against its switch, in any category, its checkbox in Units shows a dash, and Custom is lit; a press on a dash shows the whole system (Sean, 2026-10-15: until then exceptions outlasted every switch, so specialty units showed with Specialty unchecked). The unit in the box always shows. A fresh visit shows every system but Specialty (the Standard preset), so the specialty units wait under Hidden, one tap away.

**Hidden and Edit.** Hidden at the table's foot opens a list of the hidden units under their sections' names, each a checkbox with its symbol and, with Symbol and name, its name, a long name wrapping onto another line rather than cut short (Sean, 2026-10-15), set in under a three-state checkbox on its section's heading (all, some as a dash, none); no values, since the list is for bringing units back. Checking one shows it in its section. A unit that moves between shown and hidden glides to its new place, at the theme's slow motion (400 ms in Daylight) on an even curve, so the travel is seen: one checked in Hidden travels up to its row, the rows around it making room, a part new to the table fades in, and one that leaves with nowhere to go (Hidden folded) fades out where it was; it flashes the accent's soft tint as it lands, holding a moment then fading, over 1.2 s, and Hidden flashes when units go into it (Sean, 2026-10-15). What did not move on the page never slides, however the page scrolls as the table changes. The Units panel's switches and presets glide too; Display's changes rewrite the values in place. With reduced motion a unit moves at once, and the flash, a change of color only, stays. The page takes clicks while a unit moves, so units can be checked one after another. Edit lists the shown units as Hidden lists its own, a line each, a checkbox, the symbol, and, with Symbol and name, the name, set in under its section's checkbox, as a tree's child is, with no values, since Edit is for choosing units, the box's unit checked and fixed in its fill; the hidden units stay in Hidden below, open or folded as it was (Sean, 2026-10-15). So shown units are always above and hidden ones below, and a checkbox moves a unit between them wherever it is: unchecked in Edit, a row leaves for Hidden at once, as a unit checked in Hidden leaves for its row. A section's checkbox acts on the units listed under it, in Edit hiding the section's, in Hidden showing them. The table's foot stays as it was: Edit reads Done in its place, switched on (the accent's soft fill) while Edit is, Hidden staying beside it; the card stays where it was on the screen as Edit starts and ends; over a list longer than the screen the foot stays at the screen's bottom edge, Done in reach (Sean, 2026-10-14). In Edit the card is where the keys go, its edge the accent while focus is in it: ↑ and ↓ move between its headings and lines (Home and End to the first and last), never out of it; Enter or Space checks or unchecks; and after a unit moves, focus stays where it was, on the line that takes its place, so a list can be gone down unit by unit. Escape or Done ends Edit, folding Hidden, so whoever just chose what to hide need not fold it again, focus on the button that reads Edit again. Typing ends it too, as converting is the job again: in the box, from the table (where it lands in the box), or Escape in the box. A new category ends Edit. Out of Edit, the foot and Hidden take the same arrows; opening Hidden never starts Edit.

**The matches.** Once typing reaches a unit (a letter or a symbol after the number: not a sign, a decimal mark, an operator, or the "e" of an exponent on its way, `1e` before `1e5`), the table's place lists the matches (§1): ranked by §4 across every category, the current category first among equal matches, at most 20. Hidden units leave the matches; when one matches, a line under the list says so ("2 hidden units also match", or "Only hidden units match") with Show them, which lists them under that line, as the table's Hidden lists its units under the table's foot (Sean, 2026-10-15; until then they joined the matches above the line), each with its category and section ("Volume · Specialty"), under its name where the two share no line, reachable by the arrows like any match, on past the line's button; the button then reads Hide them, and folds them away again. Taking a hidden unit into the box unhides it, as if checked in Hidden, so clearing the box never hides it again. Nothing typed meets a bare "No unit matches" while a hidden unit matches. Each category's matches stay together, in the order of their best match. A typed category name offers the category itself first in its group ("Length all its units"). The letters the query matches are set heavier, under the same folding search uses (`me` marks "**me**ters" and "kilo**me**ters"; `floz` marks all of "fl oz"); a unit found through an alias the row does not show gets no mark. With no match: "No unit matches “xyz”. Try ml, feet, or °F." Typing back to a number alone brings the units or the start back.

**Placeholder.** The box says what it takes, never a value it might be mistaken for: "Number or unit" with no unit, "Number" with one.

## 4. Matching and ranking

A query is folded for matching: case, accents, spaces, periods, and a degree sign before a letter do not count, so `F` finds Fahrenheit; a degree sign alone is the angle's (`45°`), and also starts every scale whose symbol begins with one (°C, °F, °R), superscripts read as digits, and `µ` reads as `u` (`normalize.ts`). It is matched against each unit's symbol, name, plural, and aliases, and against each category's name and aliases.

Match classes, strongest first:

1. Exact symbol, case-sensitive (`mL`)
2. Exact symbol, name, plural, or alias, case-insensitive (`ml`, `kilos`, `centigrade`)
3. Prefix of a name, plural, or alias (`kilo…`)
4. Prefix of a symbol
5. Substring of a name, plural, or alias
6. One typo away, for units only, only when nothing above matched and the query has at least three characters

**Ingredients** match under Cooking, a group of their own (ADR 0021): by name or alias, exactly, from the start, or, from three letters on, inside one (`sugar` finds brown sugar). A query that ends with an ingredient named exactly reads the words before it as the unit: `2 cups flour` and `2 cups of flour` offer one match, "cups of all-purpose flour", first, which Enter takes, the unit and the ingredient together; `2 eggs` counts large eggs. The Cooking group leads when the query ends with an ingredient, in Cooking, or when an ingredient matches better than any unit (`oil` before the oil barrel); otherwise it follows the units (`o`).

**Category rows** appear only when the query starts the category's name (`vol` → Volume) or equals one of its aliases exactly (`fluids`, `temp`, `how heavy`). Aliases never match by prefix or substring, and categories are never fuzzy-matched.

**Order.** Stronger class first, except among exact matches: there the current category's comes first (in Torque, `nm` is the newton meter; in Speed, `c` the speed of light), then a key written as typed, its letters' case included, since symbols are case-sensitive (with no category chosen, `nm` is the nanometer and `Nm` the newton meter, `kn` the knot and `kN` the kilonewton; Sean, 2026-10-16), and then a standard unit before a specialty one, whichever key matched (`c` is Celsius's alias and the speed of light's symbol, both as typed: Celsius first). The unit a query names exactly is chosen the same way. Within a class, categories before units; then the current category's results first; among units, standard units before specialty ones (the catalog's tier), then the preferred unit family first (US visitors see US units first, UK visitors imperial, everyone else metric, from the browser's region), then display order, as the table lists them (§3): a US visitor's `°` offers °F, °C, then °R. So a single letter that starts a category's name offers that category first (`p`: Pressure and Power, then pounds and pints).

## 5. The box and its unit

Nothing is chosen on a keystroke, and a unit is never chosen for you, on load or otherwise.

- **Choosing a category** (the menu, a tile, or a category match) keeps the typed number, drops a typed query, and lists the category's units with none chosen: the values are dashes until a unit is picked, so a typed number means that number in the unit picked (250, then Volume, then cups, gives 250 cups, not 250 teaspoons converted). Nothing is highlighted for you either: the caret waits in the box, and ↓ or Enter moves the cursor into the units (§8). Choosing the category the box is already in keeps its unit, the cursor resting on it.
- **Taking a unit into the box.** A click or tap on a row, or Enter on it, takes its unit into the box: with its value to the 10 digits a box shows, as if it had been typed, while the table has values; with the number as typed while the rows are dashes. A match is taken the same way, with the number as typed, its category following: `5 ml` and Enter is 5 with the mL chip. One undoable step, after which every row converts from it and the cursor rests on its row. The row of the unit in the box, and a row with a message, do nothing. After a click or Enter the caret is in the box; after a tap the on-screen keyboard stays down, or goes away if it was up (§8, "On a touch screen").
- **A typed unit is only taken by Enter or a click.** Tab and leaving the box never take it: the text stays as typed, and back in the box Enter takes the best match.
- **The ingredient's pill** (Cooking, ADR 0021) follows the unit's chip in the box: the ingredient's name, a chevron, and its own ×, which takes the ingredient out and keeps Cooking, its ingredients to choose from again, the weight or volume waiting in the box (a unit of that ingredient alone, a stick of butter, goes with it; Sean, 2026-10-16: one pill's × dropping the box into Volume was jarring); Backspace in an empty box takes the last pill out first, the ingredient, then the unit, Cooking staying until a category is chosen or a unit of another category typed. A × takes one thing out and keeps its context; Escape (§9) is what backs out all the way. Pressing the pill lists every ingredient in the table's place, the box's popup, under their kinds' headings as Cooking lists them with none chosen, the one in the box marked and the cursor on it, the chevron turned over; the arrows move through them from the box, and Enter, a click, or a tap takes one, keeping the number and the weight or volume in the box; taking the one already there, Escape, typing, or the pill again closes the list unchanged (built as a trial on 2026-10-16 and kept on 2026-10-17, Sean: "really nice"; how people find what Cooking has without typing; a menu in the toolbar was tried first and wrapped the toolbar onto a second line on a desktop and a third on a phone). Opening the list moves the page nowhere, the cursor resting on the ingredient without scrolling to its row; taking one from a list the page had scrolled down brings the box back into view, as any list the table replaces does (typed matches, the tiles), while a unit taken from the table itself leaves the page where it is, its rows staying put (Sean, 2026-10-16). A press on a pill or its × moves focus nowhere: the caret stays in the field if it was there (a touch screen's keyboard with it, until a choice is made), and after a click it returns to the field without scrolling (ADR 0015). And whatever the box's table is swapped for, the box stays where it was on the screen: the page is anchored to it across every change of the table (Sean's iPhone, 2026-10-17, where a swap clamped the page to the top; docs/LEARNINGS.md), moving only where a rule above says. The box stays one line wherever it can, fitted as with one chip (Sean, 2026-10-16: on a desktop it wrapped where one line read better): the unit's chip gives up its name first, then the ingredient's pill its chevron, then the number steps down its sizes; only where the number at its smallest size still lacks the room beside both pills (a phone) do the pills take a line of their own under it, inside the box, at its right end, the unit's name and the chevron shown there, in that order, where both pills fit whole, and the ingredient's name cut short only where that line is narrower than both; the number keeps its line. Empty, the box keeps its placeholder's room.
- **The chip** reads like a row: the unit's symbol, semibold and muted, then its name (`pt pints`); with Symbol only (§7), or when the box needs the room, it shows the symbol alone, in the ink color, the name kept in its label. Room is measured in the box itself, before the value steps down a size (§7), so a wide box keeps the name beside a longer number than a phone's; with Symbol only the number has the name's room. It is a readout: clicking its body puts the caret in the box and opens nothing, the table being the list. Its × takes the unit out and keeps the category, the table staying with dashes; so does Backspace in an empty box. A click on × puts the caret in the box; a tap leaves the keyboard down.

## 6. The category

- **The category** the table lists is the box's unit's, or the one chosen before any unit (§5); the category menu names it (§1). Taking a match from another category switches to that category.
- **Cooking** (ADR 0021), after Volume in the menu and the tiles, is a lens over Weight and Volume: with an ingredient, its table lists Weight, then Volume, each the visitor's own system first (grams, kilograms, ounces, pounds; teaspoons, tablespoons, fluid ounces, cups, milliliters, liters; and the ingredient's own unit, a stick of butter, a large egg, a packet of yeast), a value that crosses from volume to weight or back going through the ingredient's density, marked ≈ ("about" for screen readers), one within a quantity exact. Under the table a line says how the cup is filled and what it weighs, the essentials for measuring the same way, and where the rest is: "All-purpose flour: spooned and leveled, 1 cup ≈ 127 g. Fill cups the same way for the values marked ≈ to hold; the sources are on the About page." (Sean, 2026-10-17: until then the line carried the spread of the weighings and their count, which the About page keeps.) Each figure is decided by one rule from the weighings that count: their median, or with fewer than three USDA's measured cups (Sean, 2026-10-16; docs/research/cooking-2.md). Chosen with no ingredient, Cooking lists every ingredient to choose from, under headings by kind, Flour, Sugar, Fats and oils, Dairy and eggs, Liquids and syrups, Baking, Grains, and Mix-ins and spreads (Sean, 2026-10-16; named as a pantry names its shelves, mass nouns in the singular and count nouns and idioms in the plural, Sean, 2026-10-17), where Enter goes in and Enter again takes one, as a category's units are gone into; a weight or a volume in the box stays. A unit of any other category, or another category chosen, leaves Cooking. The About page lists each ingredient's cup under Cooking, how it is filled, how the figure was reached (the middle of its weighings; with fewer than three, USDA's measured cups; or by density) and their spread, each weighing counted with its figure, and, where another way of filling the cup matters (dipped, sifted), what that weighs.
- **Taking the unit out keeps the category,** so its table stays in place, with dashes. Escape's last clearing step (§9) and clear all empty it, back to the start. In Cooking, taking the ingredient out keeps Cooking the same way: its ingredients to choose from, the weight or volume waiting (Sean, 2026-10-16).
- **Clear all** empties the box, its unit, and its category, back to the start, puts the caret in the box (not after a tap, §8), and when there is nothing to clear is dimmed and does nothing, keeping its place in the toolbar (§8). It can be undone like any other step.

## 7. Conversion and display

- **The table converts** whenever the box holds a number and a unit: every row shows the value in its unit as Display writes it, to 0.01 on a fresh visit. With No limit, a row shows 6 significant digits, as one past what places mean (1e15) does. A value moved into the box comes at 10 significant digits, trailing zeros trimmed, with the locale's grouping; magnitudes from 1e15 up, or below 1e-6, switch to exponent form (`1.23456789E23`).
- **Display** (Settings, §1; ADR 0018; core's `formatPlaces` and `formatFraction`). Decimals are written to every place chosen, trailing zeros too (96.00, 0.50), so a column lined up on its decimal points reads alike and says how precise each value is (Sean, 2026-10-15; trimmed before). Fractions are written as mixed numbers (`6 5/16`), "≈" before one that rounds, every fraction of each size chosen (5/64 among sixty-fourths), each in the smallest size chosen that writes it exactly: with 1/4 alone a half is 2/4, with 1/2 as well, 1/2; so the default halves, quarters, and eighths read 3/8, never 6/16. Thirds mix with the rest (1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 3/4). Small values round as chosen too (0.006 to two places is 0.01, and so is 0.001 rounded up; 0.07 to eighths is ≈ 1/8); one that is not zero but rounds to zero reads "< 0.01" or "< 1/8" (the smallest size chosen; below zero "> −0.01"), never 0.00, so it is never taken for nothing (Sean, 2026-10-15; until then such values kept their significant digits, mixing formats in a column). No limit shows six significant digits. Past a million a fraction is the whole number, rounded as chosen (≈ 1,043,262), so no row's fraction is left blank (Sean, 2026-10-15); past a quadrillion, as with places, the significant digits stand. Rounding up is toward larger values and down toward smaller, below zero too, as ceiling and floor are (−2.15 rounds up to −2.1 and down to −2.2); a tie rounds away from zero, below zero as above it (−2.5 to −3, as 2.5 to 3; Sean, 2026-10-15); float noise never rounds a value down a step (0.29 down to two places stays 0.29). Negative values take the locale's minus sign in every format. With Both, the decimal comes first and its fraction after it in the muted ink; where a row has no room for the fraction beside it (a phone, a long value), every row's fraction moves onto a second line under its decimal, a step smaller, lined up on the decimal point as the values are (`≈ 36` under `36`, then `3/4`), the symbol and the name centred beside both lines and the row keeping its 44 px (Sean, 2026-10-15).
- **Paces** are written in minutes and seconds, to the nearest second (`5:30`; `65:30` past an hour, the minutes never carried into hours, so what is written reads back the same, §2), whatever Display's places or fractions; one under a second reads `< 0:01`, never 0:00. The colon lines up with the decimal points above and below it. Copied, or moved into the box, a pace comes as written (Sean, 2026-10-16).
- **The exponent marker** in a value is semibold, in the box and in the table's rows, so `1.5E21` reads as a number with an exponent. In the box, only a marker inside the parsed number counts.
- **Running value.** While the box holds arithmetic and nothing else, its value follows it in the field in the muted color: `5 + 3 = 8`. The table converts from that value as it changes.
- **Numbers fit.** A long number steps down through five type sizes until it fits on one line beside the chip, re-measured on every change and on resize, and steps back up when room returns. Past the smallest size it scrolls inside the field. Before it steps down at all, the chip gives up its name, if that makes the room (§5).
- **Out of range.** When the typed number is beyond what a double holds (`1e400`), the table says "Too large to convert" in place of its rows; nonzero digits that round to zero say "Too small to convert", arithmetic that divides by zero "Can’t divide by zero", and a power with no real value "No real answer". A row whose own value overflows or rounds to zero says "Too large" or "Too small", and 0 in a reciprocal unit leaves the units it divides into saying "Undefined for 0" (0 mpg has no L/100 km). Never a bare 0.
- **Copy.** One copy button at a time, laid over the end of the active row, the rows keeping no column for it (Sean, 2026-10-15, from the copy lab; until then a Copy buttons setting put one on every row and the box): the row under the mouse, else the row the cursor is on, else the row of the box's unit, so on a phone a tap on a row takes it in and shows its button. A short fade of the row's own fill sets it over the end of the row's name. It copies the value as the row shows it (0.47, not 0.473176473; with Fraction its fraction, with Both its decimal), without the "≈" of one that rounds, its minus a plain hyphen so it pastes as a number. Ctrl/⌘+C copies the row whose button shows for the keys the same way, the cursor's, or while it rests, the box's unit's, as right after Enter took it (§8), the way to copy from the keys, so the button's tooltip names it ("Copy (Ctrl+C)") and the shortcuts map lists it (§10). A click on the button never takes focus from the box, as a click on a row doesn't, so the arrows go on through the rows after a copy (Sean, 2026-10-15). The other rows' buttons stay for screen readers, unseen and out of the pointer's way, so a tap at a row's end takes the row; a dash or a message has none. A successful copy swaps the icon for a check in the success color and announces "Copied". With a mouse, the check stays while the pointer rests on the button and reverts when it leaves, after at least a second; without a mouse (keyboard, touch) it reverts after two seconds. Copying elsewhere moves the check there.
- **Copy URL.** A link icon in the header, between the shortcuts map and the theme menu, named "Copy URL" (its label and tooltip), copies the URL of the current conversion (§11). The URL is built from the converter's state at the moment of the click, so it is current even inside the address bar's write delay; with nothing entered it is the bare page address. It confirms exactly like the copy buttons, and the status region says "URL copied". Unlike the shortcuts map it shows on every device, phones included. The button is drawn by the converter island into a slot the header reserves, so it appears after hydration without moving anything.
- **Announcements.** A polite live region says how many units the value is shown in once the table settles (600 ms after the last change): "250 milliliters in 9 units of volume", arithmetic named by its value ("8 milliliters in …"), the count of units that show; with dashes, how many units are listed ("9 units of volume"); while a unit is typed, how many matches there are ("5 matches", "Only hidden units match", "No unit matches"); and when there is no answer, "1e400 kilometers is too large to convert", or "Can’t divide by zero". The Units and Display panels' resets say "Hidden units shown", "Order reset", and "Display reset" in a status region of their own.

Each toolbar is one Tab stop, the control last used in it (the WAI-ARIA toolbar pattern): the header's comes first in the Tab order, then the converter's, then the box, then the side panel open (Units, Display, or the calculator, which keeps Tab going round its own stops, §8), then the table's foot (Hidden, Edit, and in Edit or the Hidden list their checkboxes; while a unit is typed, Show them): the rows, the tiles, and the matches are no Tab stops, and the box's clear, the chip's buttons, and the copy buttons are out of the Tab order because the box's own keys do the same jobs; pointers and screen readers still reach them. Escape from the page is no character key, so WCAG 2.1.4 does not reach it.

### The calculator

A scratch pad for working out a number, for anyone who would rather tap than type or wants to try a few sums first. Nothing in it touches the box until its number is sent, and it can be opened, used, and closed without sending anything.

- **Opening and closing.** The calculator icon, the first of the toolbar's tools, opens it, between the box and the table or beside them (§1); while open, the icon is switched on (accent-soft fill, `aria-expanded`). Alt+C (⌥C on Apple devices) opens and closes it from anywhere on the page, and the icon's tooltip says so. It closes with the icon, its own close button (×, top right, beside the title "Calculator"), Escape on an empty display, or Alt+C. Closed from inside it, focus goes back to where it was when the calculator opened: the icon, or where Alt+C was pressed (the box, when nothing had focus); closed from outside it, focus stays where it is. Clicking elsewhere on the page leaves it open. What it holds stays while the page is open, so reopening shows the same entry.
- **It holds the keyboard.** Opening moves focus onto `=`, so typing goes straight to it: digits, `.` or `,`, `+ - * / ^`, `e` for E, `=`, and Backspace press the matching keys, and bring focus back to `=`. The arrow keys move focus between its keys by where they sit, so it works without a mouse or a number pad: ↑ from the top row reaches close, ↓ from the bottom row reaches "Use" and ↑ from it the key it left, and nothing wraps. Clicking any key gives the keyboard back to it if focus had moved elsewhere. Clicking into the box moves the keyboard to the box, as anywhere on a page.
- **Where the keys go.** While focus is inside the calculator, its edge shows the accent, as the box's does while it has focus, so it is clear which one the keys reach; each key still shows the focus ring as Tab or the arrows reach it.
- **Enter and Tab.** Enter, or Space, presses the key with focus, so the focus ring always shows what Enter will do; after typing that is `=`, so Enter works the sum out. Tab and Shift+Tab cycle through `=`, "Use", and close; from a key the arrows reached, Tab goes on to the next of them. The other keys stay out of the Tab order. "Use" stays in the cycle even with nothing to send; it is then dimmed and does nothing (`aria-disabled`).
- **Escape** takes back the latest burst of typing (keys less than 700 ms apart), one burst at a time; after `=` or `C` it clears the display; on an empty display it closes the calculator.
- **Display.** A box the size of the converter's field, with the same type sizes, showing the entry and, for arithmetic, its running value in the muted color (`12×3 = 36`). Empty, it shows a muted 0. A long entry steps down through the sizes, then keeps its end in view.
- **No value to use.** When the entry has no value a number can hold, the display says why at its right, in the danger color: "Too large" (a large exponent such as `9^999`, or anything past about 1.8 × 10³⁰⁸, negative numbers included, since it is their size that does not fit), "Too small" (nonzero but rounding to zero, `1E−400`), "Can’t divide by zero", or "No real answer". `=` then does nothing and "Use" stays dimmed, until a key brings the entry back in range. The message is part of the description of `=` and of "Use", so a screen reader hears why.
- **Keys**, 48 px or taller, four to a row: `C ⌫ ^ ÷`, `7 8 9 ×`, `4 5 6 −`, `1 2 3 +`, `E 0 . =`. Digits are plain; `C`, `⌫`, `E`, and the decimal mark are quieter (raised surface, muted ink); the five operators are tinted; `=` is filled.
- **What they do.** Digits and the decimal mark (the locale's, `.` or `,`) add to the display, or start it over right after `=`. An operator adds to what the display shows, so a result can be carried on (`× 2`); a second operator in a row replaces the first, except `−` after `×`, `÷`, or `^`, which starts a negative number. On an empty display only `−` works. `^` raises to a power (`2^10` is 1024). `E` means times ten to the power of: `1.5E3` is 1500, `E` on its own starts `1E`, one per number, and an `E` waiting for digits takes only a sign (`1E−3`). The decimal mark is ignored when the number already has one, or is in its exponent, and starts a number as `0.`. `⌫` deletes the last character, `C` empties the display, and `=` replaces arithmetic with its value.
- **Sending.** Under the keys, "Use", with the box's unit symbol once it has one ("Use mL"), puts the display's value into the box: the running value, so `=` is optional. It replaces the number there, or fills an empty box, and keeps the box's unit and any unit text typed after the number. The button confirms with the check copy uses (§7), after its label, drawing itself in, and the status region says "Sent to the value". The calculator stays open. After a send, focus returns to `=`, so the next keys start on the display and Enter cannot send the same number twice.
- **One history with the box.** The calculator's entry is part of the converter's undo history (§9): typing in it comes in the same bursts, `=`, `C`, and each send are steps of their own, and Ctrl/⌘+Z and Ctrl/⌘+Y, or the toolbar's undo and redo, step through all of it in order. Undo after a send takes back the send, then the `=` before it, and so on. Clear all empties the box and leaves the calculator's entry alone.
- **Accessibility.** A group labelled by its title, "Calculator"; each key a native button named in words ("plus", "to the power of", "times ten to the power of", "decimal point", or "decimal comma" where the comma is the mark, "backspace", "clear"); "Use" named by what it shows. The display is plain text at the top of the group, so a screen reader reaches it first. Keys respond only while focus is inside the calculator, so its single-key shortcuts never fire elsewhere (WCAG 2.1.4).

### On a touch screen

A phone or tablet is used by tapping, and the on-screen keyboard is needed only to type a number or a unit, so the converter never brings it up for anything else (ADR 0015).

- **The keyboard comes up only from the box.** A tap in the box, on its text or on the chip's name inside it, puts the caret there and brings the keyboard up, as any text field does. Nothing else moves focus into the box after a tap: a tile, a row, a match, a choice in the category menu, the box's ×, the chip's ×, clear all, the calculator and its "Use". The box's × leaves the keyboard up if it was, to type the next number.
- **The number pad while a unit is in the box** (Sean, 2026-10-15). With a unit in the chip the box takes a number alone, so it asks for the decimal pad (`inputmode="decimal"`): digits and the decimal mark. With no unit it keeps the full keyboard, since letters type a unit (`2 cups`), and so it does in Temperature and Angle, whose values go below zero as often as not, the pad having no minus. Arithmetic and exponents need the full keyboard too: the chip's × brings it back, or the calculator does the math. A change takes effect at the next tap in the box.
- **A tap in the table puts it away.** Taking a tile, a row, or a match with a tap also puts the keyboard away if it was up, so the table being picked from stays in view. The box shows what was taken; a tap in it brings the keyboard back to change it.
- **Done finishes.** The keyboard's Enter key, labelled Done (a check on some keyboards), does what Enter does in the box (takes the typed unit's best match, works out arithmetic) and then puts the keyboard away.
- **The category menu** chosen by a tap closes with focus on its button, as the theme menu does, so a screen reader keeps its place.
- **Mouse and keys keep the desktop's flow.** This is decided by each press, not by the device: after a mouse click or a key, the caret goes back to the box as described above, so a tablet with a trackpad and a keyboard works as a desktop does, and a laptop's touch screen as a phone does.

## 9. Undo, redo, and Escape

- **Typing comes in chunks.** Keystrokes in the box less than 700 ms apart form one chunk; a longer pause, or a space, starts a new one, so `5 ml` is "5" and then " ml". The calculator's keys chunk the same way. Each chunk, each unit taken in or out, each chosen category, each clear, each send, and each `=` or `C` in the calculator is one step. Undo and redo step through them all in order (up to 50), box and calculator alike. Loading a conversion from a URL starts a fresh history.
- **Escape steps back a ladder**, one step a press:
  1. back from browsing the table, the cursor resting again (only after the arrows moved it: right after Enter or a click it already rests, so this step is skipped);
  2. a typed unit, back to the number;
  3. the number;
  4. in Cooking, the ingredient, back to its ingredients to choose from, the unit staying: one pill a step, as Backspace and the pills' × take them (Sean, 2026-10-16);
  5. the unit and the category, back to the start, as clear all does;
  6. it lets go of focus, leaving the page.

  Escape on the chip, a copy button, or a toolbar's control brings the caret back to the box.

  With nothing on the page focused, Escape first ends Edit if it is on, then takes the clearing steps, 2 to 5, one a press, without taking focus: so with something typed, a unit, or a category on screen, Escape from anywhere clears it back to the start. The cursor shows only while focus is in the box or its table, so step 1 never waits there. An Escape that closes a header menu does only that.

- Each Escape step that changes the conversion is itself a step that undo can reverse.

## 11. URL and persistence

- The URL holds the conversion: `?a=<unit id>&v=<number>`, for example `?a=milliliter&v=5`. `v` appears only when the box holds a number: the number as typed, with a period decimal and no grouping (`1,000.50` is `v=1000.50`), so every digit comes back, even past what a double holds; arithmetic is written as its value, and a time as typed (`v=5%3A30`, its colon escaped). `i` holds Cooking's ingredient by id (`?a=us-cup&v=2&i=all-purpose-flour`), an unknown one dropped. Read back, it takes the visitor's own decimal mark (`v=1234.5` opens as `1234,5` in German). It is written with `replaceState` 250 ms after the last change, and read once after the page hydrates. An unknown unit id is dropped. A link from the two-box converter, which may also carry `b` (the second box's unit) and `s` (which box held the number), opens on the box that held the number, and is then rewritten in this form (ADR 0013). A category chosen with no unit is not in the URL (Sean, 2026-10-08: not needed).
- `localStorage` holds the theme choice and the settings (what shows, Custom and the set kept for it, the sections' order, and how values look; `bigsmall:settings`, versioned, Display by what was changed, read back part by part so a unit gone since or a part that does not read falls back to a fresh visit's), and on `/dev/wordmark-lab` and `/dev/sketchbook-lab` each lab's settings. Settings stay out of the URL and of the undo history (ADR 0018). The app works when storage is blocked, settings then lasting for the visit.
- Counting (ADR 0019): on the production site, once its website ID is set, Umami counts each visit's page view, without the URL's query or hash, and these events: Unit chosen (its category and unit), Ingredient chosen (its id), Value copied (the box's unit and the row's), URL copied, Calculator opened, Calculator sent, Units opened, Display opened, Setting changed (which, and to what: the presets and Display's choices), and Theme picked (a theme kept when its menu closes, not the previews on the way). No cookies, nothing stored on the device, nothing personal sent; with the tracker blocked the page works the same.

## 12. Accessibility notes

- The box is named "Value" and described by the intro.
- The unit chip's body is named for its unit ("Unit: cups"), and its × "Remove cups".
- The box is a WAI-ARIA combobox (ADR 0006, ADR 0012): focus stays in the input, its popup is what the table's place shows, and it points at what the cursor is on only while it is there. The units are a grid (`aria-haspopup="grid"`) inside a region named by the category: each section a row group under a header row (its system's name, a column header), each unit's row holding the unit's cell, named by what it shows ("0.25 L liters") and described as moving the unit into the box, and, with a value, a cell with its copy button, seen on the active row only (§7), so no control sits inside another. Edit and the Hidden list are no grid: real checkboxes in groups named by their section, a section's three-state one on its heading. Units and Display are each a non-modal group named by its title, their buttons `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`, as the calculator's is.

Undo, redo, and clear all, when they can do nothing, are `aria-disabled`: read as unavailable, still reached by the arrows.

- Every control is at least 44 × 44 px, keyboard-operable, and visibly focused; the box shows focus with its accent edge; buttons, and the menus' triggers while closed, with the focus ring. Every theme passes the axe suite.

## 13. Edge cases

- Negative values are allowed for every unit, with no warning.
- A pasted value with a unit (`5 ml`) is parsed like typing; the unit is taken on Enter or a click, like any typed query.
- Typing before the page's code has loaded is kept. The box is a real field in the server's page, so keys typed into it stay; a letter, a digit, `-`, `.`, or `,` typed with nothing focused moves focus into it first (the inline script in `layouts/Base.astro`). As the island loads it adopts the field's text, a link's unit staying and its number giving way to what was typed. Tests still wait for the `data-hydrated` mark before typing.
