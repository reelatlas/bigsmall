# 0026 — Compound quantities: one grammar reads them, and a pair is a unit of its own

Date: 2026-10-19
Status: accepted; amended 2026-10-19 (the ninety-eighth pass)

## Context

People type a height as `5'11`, `5 ft 11 in`, or `5ft11`, a body weight as `11 st 4`, a newborn's as `6 lb 3 oz`, a duration as `1h30`, an angle as `45° 30′ 15″`: a value in two or three units of one kind, largest first. The demand map's compound worker (docs/research/demand-map-1/compound/findings.md, 2026-10-19) measured the forms: `5'11` with no inch mark leads the search completions, Google's calculator reads nearly every form, sums an overflowing part, reads `5.11 ft` as decimal feet, and drops the pair at the twelve-inch boundary; CLDR writes a height in feet and inches in the US and the UK, in metres and centimetres in France, Spain, and Italy, and a weight in stone and pounds in the UK; NIST SP 811 §7.3 allows more than one unit only for time and plane angle, and a browser's Intl cannot format a pair. Until this decision the box read `5'11"` as 5 and a query of `'11"`, which matched nothing, and the table had no row that wrote a value the way those visitors read it. Sean's call on the mock (the ninety-seventh pass): build it as drawn, the symbol column measured to the widest symbol rather than a wrap.

## Decision

- **One grammar, in core** (`packages/core/src/compound.ts`, called by `parse`): a compound is two or three parts of one pair in the pair's order, each a number and its unit, the last unit left off where the part before it has one partner (`5 ft 11`, `11 st 4`, `1h30`), a decimal, a fraction, a mixed number, a vulgar fraction, or "and a half" in any part, spacing never mattering, a comma or "and" between parts, a leading sign over the whole, and the words people type around a quantity dropped beside a number (`how tall is 180 cm`, `5'11 tall`). Marks are feet and inches (`'` and its look-alikes, `"`, `''`, the primes), and after a degree, minutes and seconds of arc; `m` and `s` are minutes and seconds only after an hour, and `d`, `m`, `s` only in an angle that starts with `d`. "in" after a number is the inch; after a unit it is left for the query, as is any last word that names no part (`5'11 cm` is 5 ft 11 in, then `cm`). A mark opens only the pair it starts, so a lone prime is feet, never an arcminute; a reading that leans on a context spelling needs every part named (`2 d 3 h` is no angle); two readings that tie are no reading. What is no compound reads as before: `5.11 ft` is decimal feet, `5-11` is arithmetic, `5 11` and `511` are a number and a query, `11 in 5 ft` is out of order, `5 ft 11 cm` is read as far as it goes and `cm` left for the query. The value is the parts summed in the last part by whole ratios, then in the pair's first; the canonical text is the pair's own words at ten significant digits (`5 ft 11 in`, `-5 ft 11.5 in`, `45° 30′ 15″`), which reads back as the same value, and is what the URL carries.
- **A pair is a unit of its own** (`pair` on the unit: the ids of its parts, largest first): `foot-inch`, `stone-pound`, `pound-ounce`, `hour-minute-second`, and `degree-arcminute-arcsecond`, each linear with its first part's factor, so a plain number in it is that part (6 in feet and inches is 6 ft), cited to the standard that fixes the parts' ratio and covered by a golden row; the registry test holds each part to the pair's category and each ratio to a whole number. Metres and centimetres wait for demand. A pair leads its group in the listed order (ADR 0011 amended), the way its system writes the quantity, and sits in the section that writes it that way (feet and inches with US customary and UK imperial, stone and pounds with the UK's, pounds and ounces with the US's, the time and angle pairs under Standard).
- **Written as its parts, the total rounded and then split** (`formatPair`): the total in the last part is rounded to Display's step, places, the fraction sizes, or the significant digits, then shared out, so 71.996 in reads `6 ft 0.00 in`, never 5 ft 12 in; every part is written, a zero remainder too, so the row's shape never changes; the sign goes before the pair; a value under the step reads below it (`< 0.01 in`); the table's own text is six significant digits of the whole, the box's ten. A pair row copies as it shows and moves into the box as its parts, the last to ten digits.
- **In the box**: a compound names its pair, offered as the match to take, first, with whatever a trailing query matches; Enter takes it, the text staying as typed (`5'11`), the chip the pair's; a unit taken from the matches while a compound is in the box takes the compound converted into it, since a quantity in two units cannot be re-read in a third. A quiet line under the box, 8 px under it in the muted ink at the small size, says what was read, normalised ("Read as 5 ft 11 in", "Read as 6 ft 2 in"), for every compound, since the visitor typed a shorthand; a decimal of feet whose decimals read as inches (5.11 ft) is read as written, as Google reads it, with the pair one press away ("Read as 5 ft 11 in instead"), one undoable step. A pair in the chip keeps the full keyboard on a touch screen and names itself in the placeholder ("Feet and inches"). The symbol column is as wide as the table's widest symbol, measured, so the pair's `ft in` keeps every name aligned (the sizer row, docs/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md "Lists").
- **What the box could not read is counted by its shape** (ADR 0019 amended): once an input with no match has stood a moment, its shape alone, digits a 9, letters an a, the marks kept, never the text, so the grammar grows from what people type.

## Alternatives considered

- **Feet and inches as a lens or a category of its own.** A height is a length; a category would split lengths in two and leave stone and pounds, hours and minutes, and angles each needing another.
- **Read a compound on the keystroke, the chip set for the visitor.** A unit is never chosen for the visitor (docs/UX-SPEC.md §5): the compound is offered as a match, and Enter takes it, as every typed unit is.
- **The pair's value in its smallest part** (71 inches). A plain number in the chip would then be inches, where "6" in feet and inches means 6 ft to anyone; the first part keeps the chip readable.
- **Split first, then round each part.** Prints 5 ft 12 in at the boundary (RapidTables' formula); Google hides the pair there instead.
- **Wrap a long symbol in its column.** It breaks the row's height and the baseline the recipe holds; measuring the column to the widest symbol was already the table's rule (Sean, 2026-10-19).
- **Sum two systems in one input** (`5 ft 11 cm`), arithmetic with compounds, a height lens, three-part lengths, metres and centimetres: tabled by demand (docs/research/demand-map-1.md §5, decision 3).

## Consequences

- `parse` gains `compound`, and the number lexer moved into `number.ts`, shared by the parser and the reader without a cycle. Every caller of `parse` sees a compound's value in the pair's first part; the calculator's send replaces the whole compound with its number.
- The listed order gains a rule (a pair leads its group), pinned in core's search tests; the first row of Length for a US visitor is feet and inches.
- The URL's `v` may now hold spaces and a unit's words (`v=5+ft+11+in`); a link read back takes the visitor's decimal mark in every part.
- The connective form (`5 ml to cups`, docs/UX-SPEC.md §14) stays planned: a compound's trailing `to cm` is a query that matches nothing until it lands.
- The pair pages of the SEO plan (docs/SEO.md §5) can open on a pair (`/feet-and-inches-to-cm/`, the heights grid), the box reading what the page's visitor types.
