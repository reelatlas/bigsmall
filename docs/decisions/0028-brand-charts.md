# 0028 — The brands' shoe-size charts as the second value: one foot, every brand

Date: 2026-10-20
Status: accepted

## Context

ADR 0027 built Shoe size on ISO/TS 19407's rule, the foot's length in millimetres, and left a brand's chart for later as "the second value, in the Cooking pill's shape". The brands' charts research (docs/research/shoe-sizes-2.md; the `orchestrate` skill's three waves, 2026-10-20) read twenty brands' own charts on their own sites, 186 charts and 16,958 cells, checked blind, and measured what the charts say: two families (a barleycorn grid, 4.2 mm a half size, at adidas, HOKA, Nike, Clarks, Reebok, and Birkenstock, which put a US men's 10 at ISO's 271 mm foot; a centimetre grid, 5 mm a half size, at New Balance, Vans, Timberland, UGG, Crocs, Dr. Martens, Under Armour, Asics, and Salomon, which put it at 28 cm, Converse's Chucks at 28.5), the brands' EU a size above ISO's beside a US men's size, UK and women's conventions that differ by brand, Nike's men's and women's pages two lasts, and every brand calling its chart approximate. A first mock drew one brand's chart beside ISO's on each scale; Sean's read (the hundred-and-sixteenth pass) was that a visitor's question is one foot across every brand, as Cooking's ingredient opens every unit ("I'm a US 9; what am I in Nike, in every brand? I measured my foot; what am I everywhere?"), and that two columns made ISO a third party where someone who has always worn Nike wants Nike as the baseline. An interactive prototype on the record (`docs/research/shoe-sizes-2/mocks/brand-proto.py`) carried the rethought shape, and he took it: "a much better shape … pretty close … go with it so we can test it more fully and see where we need to tweak it" (the hundred-and-seventeenth pass). His follow-up the same evening set the model's last piece: one value in focus. "If I type US M 11, that is the ISO measurement, and we see all values converted against it; only one is selected. If I now select a brand shoe size, that becomes the new focal value, and we do not represent it twice. If I click on Nike 11 and that differs from US M 11, I need to see the difference: the Nike value is highlighted and everything converts against that; we don't all of a sudden update all the shoe sizes to match the brand values." So a brand's chart never fills the scale rows: it gives the foot, and ISO's scales and every other brand convert from it.

A brand's chart is a conventional fact (docs/RESEARCH.md §1, ADR 0022): the brand's own table, cited to its page and date, named as the brand's, never averaged with another's or with ISO's.

## Decision

1. **A brand is the second value beside the shoe scale, in the ingredient pill's shape** (ADR 0021): typed with the size at either end (`nike 10`, `10 nike`, `nike women 8`, `eu 43 new balance`, `27 cm nike`, `nike 10 to eu`), or taken from a brand's row in the table, or from the list its pill opens, as Cooking's ingredients are. A bare size at a brand is US men's, as a bare "us" is (ADR 0027), offered as the match Enter takes with the brand ("US men · Nike"), the unit shown before it is taken; a brand alone brings no scale. The pill's × takes the brand out, the scale staying; Backspace on an empty box takes the pill out first, then the scale; Escape steps the same way. A brand stays with a shoe scale and leaves with any other unit or category; the URL carries it by id (`brand=nike`), as it carries the ingredient. There is no setting for it (Sean, 2026-10-20: no setting for one category). With a brand in the box the unit's chip draws the pill's chevron and opens the brand's scales in the table's place, every scale its chart prints and the foot's units where it gives the foot, the one in the box marked, one taken reading the same row on that scale with the brand staying (amended 2026-10-20, the hundred-and-nineteenth pass; Sean: "if I'm US M 11 adidas and now want to switch to EU, I have to click the European row, then back to the adidas row"); without a brand the chip is a readout, the table being the list.
2. **One value is in focus, and everything converts from its foot.** A size on ISO's scales, as before; or a brand's size, whose row is then the box's own, the only row filled, and whose chart gives the foot (the row's, where the brand says its length is the foot's: sixteen brands). The scale rows are always ISO 19407's rule, from that foot, none of them the box's own while a brand's size is: Nike's 10 is a 27.1 cm foot by its chart, so US men reads ≈ 10, UK ≈ 9, EU ≈ 42.5; New Balance's 9 is a 27 cm foot, so US men reads ≈ 10, the difference in view. A brand's own cells never fill the scale rows (Sean: "we don't all of a sudden update all the shoe sizes to match the brand values"); a brand's labels on another scale are its row once that scale is in focus (the chip's list: Nike's EU 44 for the same row, the brand staying; ISO's EU row tapped would read a different row the second time). The chart line is state (amended 2026-10-20): a US women's size says women's, and the line is kept as the box moves across the genderless scales, EU, UK, JP, and the foot's units, which say no line, so a women's reading stays women's where the men's chart answered before; a US men's size or another category clears it; the URL carries it (`line=women`). A brand that gives no foot length (Puma, On, Skechers, Brooks) has its size read as ISO's label on the same scale, said in the line under the table with what the brand prints for it ("Puma gives no foot length, so its 10 is read as ISO’s US men 10; it prints UK 9, EU 43 for it"); a size off the chart leaves the rows dashes and the line says the chart's span. A length typed is the value in focus itself, the brand's row its size at that foot.
3. **One row per brand under the scales, at the same foot**, a section of its own ("Brands, the same foot in US M"), with every brand in the first batch by name: the brand's size for the foot in focus, read from its own chart at the nearest row (a tie to the longer foot, as brands advise sizing up between sizes), on the scale the box's size is written on (US men's for a length; the women's charts and columns with a US women's size), ≈ where that row's foot is not the foot, as the brand prints it (a range as its two ends, `9–9.5`; a third as `44 2/3`; each standing whatever Display says, split at its first number's end as every value is at its mark, so the first number lines up with the whole numbers; amended 2026-10-20, the hundred-and-nineteenth pass, from written across, which lined up with nothing); after the name, in the muted ink, the chart's line where the scale does not say it ("men" on EU, nothing on US M or US W; amended 2026-10-20, the hundred-and-nineteenth pass: the line and the foot stood there, "men’s · 27 cm", cut short on every screen, the foot saying what ≈ and the foot's rows say, and the possessive against the units' names' form), or why the brand has no size (no foot length; prints no EU; off its chart). A brand's row taken brings its size and its brand into the box, the new value in focus; a scale's row taken with a brand in the box takes the brand out, the scales being ISO's. The section repeats the scales in another form, so Edit leaves it, its rows are never hidden on their own, and it has no switch in Units.
4. **The line under the table is facts, never fit advice** (Sean: words risk a double negative beside numbers that show the direction): with no brand, ISO's rule, that each brand's row is its own chart at that foot, and how to anchor, a brand one fits or the foot measured heel to toe in cm; with a brand, whose chart, where, and when ("Nike’s size chart, the men’s, nike.com, read Oct 2026."), the fact its numbers cannot show (Birkenstock's whole EU sizes, Crocs' pairs, Dr. Martens' silhouettes, Reebok's missing UK and EU, Converse's Chuck Taylor table, Clarks' two pages joined), where a size is off the chart its span, and where the brand gives no foot length how its size is read and what it prints for it. No link to manage: the About page lists every chart with its page, its date, its scales and sizes, and what its length is.
5. **Japan's symbol is `JP`, and the foot has a unit of its own in centimetres.** Brands print the Japan or CM column as a label about a centimetre above the foot (Nike's 28 beside a 27.1 cm foot; On's FAQ), so a Japanese row headed `cm` beside a Mondopoint row in mm read as a contradiction; the scale keeps its name and takes the scale's symbol, as `EU` and `UK` do. A typed `cm` in Shoe size is the foot's length, `foot-cm` (Foot length, `cm`, ten mm, exact, a tenth of a centimetre its step), beside Mondopoint, so a measured foot is typed as people measure it and a brand's foot shows to the millimetre; the Japanese scale's `cm` keys are its. Ten units.
6. **The charts are data written from the record, held to it by a test.** `docs/research/shoe-sizes-2/write_brands.py` writes `packages/core/src/units/shoe-brands-data.ts` from `brands.json`, the record `brands.py` writes from the keepers (the workers' cells, Sean's capture, his pasted texts), every cell as printed, the foot only where the brand's words make the length the foot's; the brands' names, aliases, shorthand keys ("on", "nb", "ua", "docs", which count only where the category is named, as a unit's `shorthand` does), sites, and the note's facts are typed once in `src/units/shoe-brands.ts`; `test/shoe-brands.test.ts` reads the record and holds core to it cell for cell. The lookups are core's (`shoe-brands.ts`: a brand read at the box, a scale's value, every brand at a foot, the grammar); the rows and the words are the web app's (`state.ts`, `selectShoe`).

## Alternatives considered

- **The brand's chart beside ISO's, a second column per scale** (the first mock). Confusing (Sean): ISO became a third party in every row, and the question people bring, one foot across every brand, had no answer.
- **The brand's own cells in the scale rows, two rows highlighted** (the prototype and the first build of this evening: Nike's 10 showing Nike's UK 9 and EU 44 in the scale rows, the scale's row and the brand's both filled). Sean, trying it: a brand's size is one focal value, everything converts against it, and the sizes must not change to the brand's; a brand's EU is its row once EU is in focus.
- **Brand against brand by label** (a Nike 10 is a New Balance 10). What every site does and what the charts say is wrong by half a size to a size; the foot is the only bridge both charts give.
- **A hint per brand under the table** (the research's first stage). The deltas are stair steps and slopes, not constants, so a sentence could only say "about a size"; the rows say it exactly (Sean's call of the hundred-and-tenth pass).
- **A Settings picker for the brand.** A setting for one category, against Sean's rule; the table is the picker, as it is the unit list (ADR 0012, ADR 0013).
- **Fit advice in the note** ("Converse says go down a half size"). Beside Converse's higher EU and UK labels it read as a contradiction; the rows show the direction, so the note keeps to facts.
- **Japan kept as `cm`.** Nike's 28 beside 271 mm in the next row was the contradiction Sean flagged; a label is a scale.
- **A `women` word with a genderless scale** (`eu 39 women`): not read yet; the line it would set is state since 2026-10-20 (decision 2), kept from a US women's size, which a tap on the US W row and then the chip reaches. Later, if asked for.
- **Hiding a brand's row, a brand's second line** (Converse's Standard table), **widths** (Mondopoint's second number; New Balance's, HOKA's, and UGG's width tables in the record), **the second batch of brands**: logged (docs/PLAN.md §2, docs/UX-SPEC.md §14), after this ships and is tried.

## Consequences

- The converter's state gains a brand beside the unit, and the chart line it is read on (2026-10-20); the box's second pill, the matches, the URL, undo, and the analytics ("Brand chosen") learn it. A table row gains `brand`, `detail`, and `printed`; a brand's name reacts to the columns as a pair's does (`PAIR_NAME_CELL`), so the single units keep sizing them; the formulas' tag is a recipe now (`TAG`).
- Core gains `shoe-brands.ts`, the brands and their charts, and the foot's cm unit; the About page lists 33 charts under Shoe size with their pages and dates; `pnpm check` holds core to the record.
- Easier: the next brand is a chart in the record and a line in `shoe-brands.ts`; apparel sizing (docs/PLAN.md §2) is the same shape, a body measurement as the base and a brand's chart as the pill.
- To watch, in Sean's hands on the live site: a brand with no foot length read as ISO's label; a kid's size at a brand (nothing); a brand's labels on another scale reached from the chip (2026-10-20); the brands' section's length on a phone; a brand typed with a typo (no fuzzy match for brands).
