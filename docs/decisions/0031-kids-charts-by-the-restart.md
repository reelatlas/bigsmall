# 0031 — A brand's kids' chart: one per brand composed from its age bands, its rows split at the restart onto ISO's children's and adult scales, read where the adults' charts stop

Date: 2026-10-21
Status: accepted

## Context

The brands' charts in the product were the adults' (ADR 0028 to ADR 0030): one or two per brand, 34 in all. The record held 70 kids' charts beside them, every brand but Brooks printing one or more (docs/research/shoe-sizes-2.md §7), read and never used: a child's foot or a children's size left every brand row a dash (`off its chart`), and the children's scales were never offered in the chip's list. Sean's data strategy (the hundred-and-eighteenth pass, 2026-10-20) asked for the kids' data "stored in a stable, systemized way that fits our shape, or tells us to expand it", and his go after the sources pass (the hundred-and-thirty-fourth pass, 2026-10-21) was to keep pushing forward.

Three things make a kids' chart unlike an adults'. A brand prints it in age bands (Nike's Babies & Toddlers, Little Kids, and Big Kids; Vans' four; Timberland's four), the bands overlapping by a few sizes. Its US and UK columns run to 13.5 and start again at 1, so a chart holding 1 to 13.5 and then 1 to 7 is ambiguous number by number, the brands printing the letter (10.5C, 1Y, J1, 13K) on some pages and not others. And ISO/TS 19407 gives children's sizes a table of their own (its Table 3, the product's US C, UK C, and EU C) up to that restart, youth sizes from it being men's sizes on the adult scales (ADR 0027).

## Decision

1. **One kids' chart per brand, composed by the record from its home site's age bands.** `brands.py`'s `KIDS` table names each brand's parts in age order with their options: a band that starts past the restart (`youth`), a band whose inches are its foot (`inches`), paired rows left out (`pairs`), a page joined onto the rows on a column the pages share (`on`, `only`), and a cell left out with why (`out`). The bands are appended, a size printed twice kept once where the bands agree, their cells unioned (Nike's 8C to 10C and 1Y to 3Y, Vans' 2C to 4C, UGG's 3T to 12T, Timberland's 4, Converse's 10C and 10.5C, Reebok's 3: all equal within a millimetre), the first kept and the disagreement recorded where they differ (none did). Clarks' GB kids' chart takes the AU page's US sizes on the UK size they share; Birkenstock's EU page takes the GB page's UK on the foot the two print alike and the US page's pairs on the EU size. The chart is named `Kids, composed` and flagged for the product as the adults' are; Brooks prints none. Nineteen charts, 528 rows.
2. **A row's scale is told by the restart, not by its number.** The rows before the first row whose US size drops below the previous row's are children's and go on ISO's children's scales, written as `us_kids`, `uk_kids`, and `eu_kids` in the record and `usKids`, `ukKids`, and `euKids` in the file; the rows from it are youth and go on the adult scales, `us_men`, `uk`, and `eu`, youth sizes being men's sizes. The UK column restarts by its own drop, a row later than the US at most brands (a 1Y is a UK 13.5), and the European column, one scale with no restart, is written by the row's kind, found from its US, else its UK. A band that starts past the restart is flagged by hand, never found by a threshold on EU or the foot, since one brand's 13.5C is longer than another's 1Y. An adults' page's kids column (Nike's youth labels beside its men's sizes, Birkenstock's template) stays in the record and never reaches the product, the writer taking the children's keys from a kids' chart alone.
3. **The grid test applies (ADR 0030).** Nike's Little Kids band prints a cm column that is its CM/JP label (140 at 8C, against 150 on the Babies band and 15.08 in on its own inch column), stepping a round centimetre a size, while its inch column steps the barleycorn and agrees with the neighbouring bands where they overlap: that band's inches are its foot, by the test and not by the heading. The length's basis is the brand's adults' (ADR 0030): the foot at Nike, adidas, UGG, HOKA, Timberland, On, Converse, Crocs, Reebok, Salomon, Clarks, and Birkenstock; the standard centimetre label at New Balance, Under Armour, and Vans, read by ISO's children's label through their own rows (tier 4); Asics's CM label less a centimetre by its rule (tier 2); none at Puma, Skechers, and Dr. Martens (its Japan column a label), tier 4. The tiers and the rows' words are the adults' (docs/RESEARCH.md §9).
4. **Cells left out, said where.** New Balance's UK 5 printed at a 2.5Y, between UK 1.5 and 2.5, out of the column's order, left out of the product chart until the refresh checks it; UGG's paired baby rows (0/1, 2/3, 4/5, 6/7, its booties), a second table inside the baby chart; Dr. Martens' first row printed twice, kept once. The research doc's kids' table (docs/research/shoe-sizes-2.md §9) lists them.
5. **The kids' chart answers where the adults' charts stop.** In the box, a children's size reads it (`nike kids 10`, `nike 10c`, `us c 10 nike`); a size on an adult scale reads the adults' chart first and the kids' where the adults' lacks it and the kids' prints it, on the scale or its twin (a Nike 3 is its 3Y, an EU 28 its 11C); a length reads the adults' chart within its reach, else the kids'. The twins are each children's scale and the adult one it restarts into: US C and US M, UK C and UK, EU C and EU. A children's size clears the women's line, as a US men's size does. The brands' rows at a foot read the adults' chart for the line, and the kids' chart where the foot is off it, the size on the scale asked or its twin (a kids' row's US C for a foot asked in US M, the symbol saying so, no `prints no` word), the row saying `kids` on every scale, since a youth size on US M is a men's size by ISO and the kids' page's by the brand; a brand with no kids' chart stays `off its chart`. The note names the chart ("Nike’s size chart, the kids’, nike.com, read Oct 2026."), and a children's size past it says its span ("Nike’s kids’ chart runs US C 1 to 13.5.").
6. **The chip's list by chart.** Under the chart in play's heading, the row's own scales (a kids' row's children's ones, or its youth ones, US W among them where the kids' page prints a women's column); then the brand's other charts under their own headings: the other gender line's own US scale beside an adults' chart whatever the foot, as before, and any other scale a chart prints only where that chart has a row at the foot in play, so a child's foot lists the kids' scales and an adult's none; every chart's scales while choosing; no scale listed twice.

## Alternatives weighed

- **A chart per age band**, the product choosing the band by the size or the foot: more charts carrying the same cells, the overlaps answered twice, and the restart still inside the Little Kids band.
- **One chart per brand, kids and adults together**: the US column would hold 1 to 13.5 and then 1 to 16 with the restart inside, so a 4 would be a baby's 4C or a youth 4Y, and the kids' page's rows would need a kind of their own anyway.
- **A kids line kept in the URL and state as the women's is**: not needed; the children's scales say the line, and a youth size reaches the kids' chart by the adults' chart lacking it.
- **A threshold on EU or the foot to split the rows**: Converse's 13.5C is 198 mm and Vans' 1Y 190, so no threshold holds across the brands; the restart in the printed column does.

## Consequences

Easier: a child's foot in centimetres, or a children's size at one brand, gives every brand's kids' size at the same foot, as an adult's does; the youth sizes continue the men's scale without a seam. The charts' file holds 53 charts and 1,226 rows, fetched once (ADR 0029). Harder: nineteen more charts to refresh before launch and yearly (docs/TESTING.md); the kids' pages were read by the workers, Sean, and the browser at the charts pass and never checked cell by cell by a checker, the composition's own checks (the overlaps equal, the columns in order, the joins complete) standing in until the refresh. Kept for Sean's eye: the row word `kids` and the headings' wording, with every string at the strings pass.
