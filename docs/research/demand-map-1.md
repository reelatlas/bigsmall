# The demand map: categories, compound input, shoe sizes, and the pair list, researched in waves

v0.1, 2026-10-19. The pass that decides what bigsmall builds before the pair pages: which categories are missing or need regrouping, how a value in two units (a height) is read and shown, how shoe sizes join under a standard, and which pairs get pages, controls included. It is the first full run of "a planner and agents that build" (docs/PLAN.md §2): an orchestrator's brief, parallel workers, checkers apart from gatherers, handoffs as files, a budget and a stop at every wave, as the `orchestrate` skill says (`.claude/skills/orchestrate/`). The method for each fact is docs/RESEARCH.md: a figure by its kind (§1), a practice in layers (§8).

## 1. What it answers

Five questions, one worker each in wave 1, every claim checked in wave 2, the decisions numbered for Sean in wave 3 (§5).

1. **Demand.** Which conversions people search, by category and by pair, in relative terms from public signals, since Keyword Planner is not set up (Sean, 2026-10-19: later); where Google's own box answers and where it does not; where our edge pairs sit against the head terms.
2. **Categories and the IA.** What a general converter should have that we lack, what to merge, split, or group, and how twenty to twenty-five categories stay quick on the start and in the menu. Candidates: shoe sizes, oven gas marks, blood sugar, paper sizes, data rate, frequency, density, flow, acceleration, typography; garment size labels and tailor measurements weighed and, if no body stands behind them, set aside with the reason.
3. **Shoe sizes.** The standards (ISO/TS 19407, ISO 9407), the systems and their arithmetic on last length, the variance, how the best sites present them, the grammar people type ("us 11", "m us 11", "eu 45", a bare "size 11"), and a model our engine can hold: affine scales as Temperature's, half-size rounding, ≈ as Cooking's densities carry, a gender or system as the second value as an ingredient joins a unit. Sean, 2026-10-19: in, "a fun and cool thing we should figure out how to include".
4. **Compound quantities.** One grammar for a value in two units of one kind, heights first (`5'11"`, `5 ft 11 in`, `5ft 11`, `6'`), then stone and pounds, pounds and ounces, hours and minutes, degrees-minutes-seconds; the noise words ("tall"); the connective form the spec plans (`5 ft 11 in to cm`); the pitfalls (5.11 is not 5'11"); and the display of a pair (5′ 11″). Sean, 2026-10-19: "these kind of conversions will give us the biggest edge".
5. **The pair list and the page's shape.** What ranking converter pages are built of and what Google's policies reward or penalize, from primary sources; the common head terms as controls, measured as a group against the edge pairs (Sean, 2026-10-19); the evidence for the page's shape, which the orchestrator drafts as a spec and an artifact mock alongside (docs/SEO.md §3, step 2).

## 2. Method

- **Facts by kind** (docs/RESEARCH.md §1): a factor or a size table is defined or conventional and cited to its body; a figure nobody defines goes through the measured rule.
- **Practices in layers** (docs/RESEARCH.md §8): our own evidence first, then the standards and the established practice (normative apart from guidance), the community and its thinkers, the wild opened and tried, the one-offs; every finding carries its layer and its evidence level (measured, reasoned, asserted).
- **Roles** (docs/RESEARCH.md §4, "How the work is split"): the orchestrator writes the briefs and decides; workers gather, one question each, none seeing another's work; checkers read the cited sources themselves, never the workers' notes; handoffs are files.
- **Safety, in every brief**: downloads in the worker's own folder and scripts elsewhere, readers run with `python -I`; nothing personal in any request; no bot check or paywall bypassed, a source behind one is "could not reach"; nothing posted or submitted anywhere; the repo read, never written.

## 5. Decisions

Filled in wave 3, numbered, Sean's and the orchestrator's, each with where to see it, what changes, and one recommendation. Positions taken so far, before wave 2's check:

- **Compound quantities** (Sean, 2026-10-19: "whatever is logical and will be ideal"). Reading: the compound worker's grammar (§7 of its report) plus a decimal, a fraction, a mixed number, or "and a half" in any part, spacing never mattering, and an inference rule that prefers the reading forming a valid compound of one system, else the decimal or arithmetic reading, with what was read shown in the quiet line and the other reading one press away. Showing: a pair is a display row, never a category; core holds a pair unit per category (ft in; st lb; lb oz; h min s; degrees, minutes, seconds; later m cm), placed in the system section that uses it by CLDR's regional conventions, its value the pair's text with the fraction setting on the last part, the total rounded before it is split, Copy and the URL carrying it; so every direction falls out of the one table. Tabled by demand: arithmetic with compounds, input mixing two systems, a height lens, three-part lengths. The look is decided on an artifact mock after wave 2. The demand data under-counts these forms, since a form that fails today is typed less (Sean), so the grammar is designed from the full plausible set and our own unread inputs are counted by shape class, never by text.
- **Shoe sizes**: the research skill's rule for a conventional fact, ISO 19407's table the anchor, brands named as brands with the variance noted, every converted size ≈, the women's offset ISO's with the +1.5 convention in a line; Sean's call whether that becomes a setting.
- **Pair pages**: the shape the pair-pages worker recommends, kept clean of the five patterns it names, a first batch small enough to review by hand, the heights page as one grid with an anchor per height before any page per height.
- **The IA**: judged on an artifact mock after wave 2, on the recommended pattern.

## 7. Wave 1: what the workers found

The headlines, each worker's report the detail (`docs/research/demand-map-1/<worker>/findings.md`), every claim with its source in the worker's `claims.csv`. Unverified until wave 2.

1. **Demand.** About a dozen head pairs lead (kg to lbs, cm to inches, mm to inches, °C to °F, in the hundreds of thousands to 1.8 million a month, third-party estimates), and Google answers every one. An edge search is 3 to 6 % of a head term on its own but comes in families of 20 to 40 values: heights ("5'10 in cm" about 90,000, the prime form leading, and Google answering neither the bare "5 10 in cm" nor the reverse without 64ths of an inch), a cup of an ingredient in grams (about 400,000 across its phrasings, none answered by Google), paces, torque (its top bucket beats pressure's, and Google misreads "nm" as nanometres), shoe sizes, gas marks, blood sugar, and paper sizes. Searches for one value are as large as mid-tier pairs and belong in a page's rows. The ten controls: kg to lbs, cm to inches, mm to inches, celsius to fahrenheit, inches to cm, km to miles, meters to feet, ml to oz, inches to mm, lbs to kg.
2. **Categories and the IA.** Add seven for 23: Shoe size, Data rate, Frequency (rpm inside it), Density, Flow, Acceleration, Blood sugar (glucose, then HbA1c); Ring size a strong 24th, later. Fold as sections: gas marks into Temperature (no body behind them, so the conventional rule), pt, pc, and CSS px into Length (CSS defines them as lengths), paper sizes into Length once a pair displays. Garment labels out: ISO 8559-2 designates clothes by body measurement and leaves the garment to its maker, so no conversion exists; tailor measurements are lengths already converted. For the start and the menu: a fixed Everyday block of eight first, one level of groups at most and only where there is room (a three-column desktop menu), no reorder by use (the menu studies: static fastest, adaptable preferred, adaptive slower), favorites later as pins. Three of the converters opened fail WCAG's reflow at a phone's width.
3. **Shoe sizes.** ISO 19407 is the body (the 2015 technical specification read in full through its Russian adoption, replaced in 2023 by a standard whose text is paywalled). Every adult system is an affine scale on foot length in millimetres, which the engine already does for Temperature: EU, UK, US men's, US women's, Mondopoint, Japan; half-size rounding; every converted value ≈. The formulas recompute 321 of the standard's own 324 table values, each miss within 0.01 size of a rounding boundary. The disputes are mapped: the US women's offset is +1 (ISO, Brannock), +1.5 (Nike, Clarks, KEEN), or +2 (Zappos's FAQ); brands mark EU half a size to a size above ISO. Children from ISO's table, never the formula. Gender as units (US men's and US women's, as mpg US and UK are), the chart as the second value (ISO by default, a brand's later, named as the brand's). A bare "us 11" reads as men's.
4. **Compound quantities.** One grammar of summed parts reads every form found: a value is two or three parts of one kind and system, largest first; the last unit may be left off where the first has one partner (`5'11`, `11 st 4`, `1h30`); a leading sign covers the whole; a connective or a trailing bare unit names the target, "in" the inch right after a number and the connective after a unit; noise words dropped. `5'11` with no inch mark leads. Pairs: ft and in, st and lb, lb and oz, h, min, and s, degrees, minutes, and seconds, m and cm. Pitfalls settled: `5.11 ft` is decimal feet with the pair offered quietly; `5-11` is arithmetic; inches past twelve are summed and shown normalized; the display rounds the total first and then splits, so `6 ft 0 in` and never `5 ft 12 in`. The table writes `5 ft 11 in`, primes only for angles; CLDR and Wikipedia disagree on commas and primes, recorded. Google reads nearly every height form and shows the pair in 64ths; most converters avoid parsing with two boxes.
5. **Pair pages.** Google's own words draw the line: a doorway "funnels visitors into the actual usable or relevant portion of a site"; scaled abuse is "substantially similar" pages outside "a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy"; no word count. The forty ranking pages are plainer than the guidance: a converter at the top, an 11 to 22 row table, a formula, a reverse link, 500 to 1,100 words, and only 5 of 40 cite a standard, none of the big sites. For a height, all five top results are pages for that one height, the one place the no-page-per-value rule meets real demand; the worker recommends one feet-and-inches page with a full grid and an anchor per height, read against Search Console first. The recommended shape: the pair as the h1, the cited factor as the answer line, the box and table opened on the pair, a values table of 15 to 25 searched values with fractions for inches, the formula and one example, the pitfalls real for the pair, links to the reverse pair and related pairs, a category hub page above, BreadcrumbList, flat addresses (`/cm-to-inches/`), 150 to 400 words written for the pair, no FAQ or HowTo markup (Google no longer shows them), and a first batch small enough to review by hand.
