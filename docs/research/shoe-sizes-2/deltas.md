The 32 charts recommended for the product (§5, decision 1), one per brand and gender line; the other 56 adult charts kept (a brand's other regions, lines, and pages) follow in `deltas.md`, and the 60 kids' charts and 5 others (socks, sandals, boots) are in `brands.json` alone. Length: what the brand says its length column is (§7, §8). mm a half size: the chart's foot step, the median over its rows; ISO's barleycorn is 4.2, a centimetre grid 5. Foot at US M 10: the brand's foot length for its US men's 10 (a women's chart: US women's 8) and its difference from ISO's 271 mm (245.6 for W 8); a positive number means the chart gives a longer foot the same size, so a measured foot gets a smaller size from the brand than from ISO. EU − ISO and UK − ISO: the brand's European or British size beside its US size, minus the size ISO's rule gives that US size, the range over the chart's rows in the range most adults buy in (US men's 6 to 13, US women's 5 to 11, UK 4 to 12, EU 36 to 47) and the mean in brackets; a visitor who types a US size and reads our EU row meets this difference. US size − ISO at the same foot: the brand's US size for a row's foot length minus ISO's, for the charts with a length; the mirror of the foot column, in sizes. W − M: the brand's US women's minus US men's on one row; ISO's is 1. A range's middle stands in for a range (Birkenstock's US sizes, Crocs' EU pairs); Clarks' composed charts are its GB and US pages joined on the UK size they share.
