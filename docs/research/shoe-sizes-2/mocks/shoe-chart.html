Shoe sizes against ISO: the engine’s chart, the brands’ own charts, and two ways to show a brand

ISO 19407 brings every scale to one base, the foot’s length in millimetres; the converter holds its rule. Each brand publishes its own chart, and where the brand gives a foot length per size, its chart can be laid over ISO’s: the difference, in sizes, is the delta. Everything below is computed from the cells the two workers recorded on the brands’ own sites; nothing is checked yet.

3. The deltas across each chart

Each strip is one chart laid over ISO: the foot along the bottom (mm), and how many sizes above or below ISO’s marking the brand’s sits, per scale. A flat line is a constant offset; a stair step is a brand marking in 5 mm grid steps against ISO’s 4.2; a slope is a brand whose sizes run bigger or smaller as the foot grows. The line under each strip is the mean over 220 to 300 mm, with the range in brackets: what a one-line hint could say.

5. What the data allows

  • Eight brands give a foot length per size and say it is the foot’s (Nike, Under Armour, Reebok, Clarks, Birkenstock, Salomon, Crocs, Dr. Martens): a chart can be shown and a delta computed for each. Reebok has US sizes only.
  • Three give a length of unstated meaning (Asics, Converse, Puma): drawn above with that caveat; a chart could still be shown as the brand’s, a delta against ISO only if the length is the foot’s.
  • Two give offsets alone (Skechers, Brooks): a chart, no delta.
  • Seven could not be reached on any of their sites (Adidas, New Balance, Vans, UGG, Hoka, Timberland, On).
  • Before any of it ships, wave 2’s checker re-reads the cells from the pages (the fetcher-read ones first), and the kept charts go into brands.py, from which the code and this page are written.
