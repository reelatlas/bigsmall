# Strings 1: the Shoe size strings and states pass, the dry run of the launch inventory

v0.1, 2026-10-21. The first pass over every piece of text a category shows, run on Shoe size at the end of its build (ADR 0027 to ADR 0031) as the dry run of the inventory before launch (docs/TESTING.md, "Before launch"; Sean's ask, 2026-10-21, the hundred-and-twenty-third and twenty-ninth passes), so the method is tried on one category before it runs over the whole product. His go for it: the hundred-and-thirty-fifth pass; the tidy routine settled beside it (the hundred-and-thirty-sixth), the tidy of the whole shoe stretch to follow this pass.

## 1. What it answers

Every state Shoe size can be in and the words each shows, where, in one place for Sean's eye and rewrite: the box and its pills, the matches, the scales' rows, the brands' rows, the line above the rows and the line under the table, the lists in the table's place, the live region's sentences, the About page's lines, and the strings elsewhere that name shoes. What comes out: his words applied with the tests pinning them, and the practices that carry over to the other categories (§6), the inventory before launch then running by them.

## 2. Method

- **Gathered from three places, each checked against the others:** the state tests (`apps/web/test/converter-state.test.ts`, "shoe sizes" and "brands"), the spec (docs/UX-SPEC.md §5, Shoe size), and the strings in the code (core's scales and brands, the converter's state, the components' labels, the About page's builder, the feedback form). The About page's lines are read from the built site (`apps/web/dist/about/index.html`) and kept in `strings-1/about-lines.json`, so the inventory shows them as rendered, not as the builder's template.
- **The inventory is data.** `strings-1/inventory.py` holds every item with its key, where it shows, the words today, the file that holds them, and a literal the generator asserts is still in that file, so a string changed in code fails the generator until the inventory follows; the scales' names, symbols, notes, and sources, the brands' names, sites, and facts, and the charts' spans and cells are read from core's own files and the charts' data, never typed here. It writes `strings-1/inventory.html`, the page for his eye, and §3's table between the markers below. The examples' sizes are illustrative; the words are exact.
- **The page saves his words to its own store** as he types (the artifact's database, one document per key under `rewrites`), and a Copy button puts them on the clipboard for chat; an empty field keeps the words. The session reads the store back (the `ArtifactData` tool) and applies them, each with its test.
- **Keys.** Each item's key names its surface and its state (`brands.off`, `note.span`); his rewrite is applied by key, so the page, the doc, the commit, and the tests say the same thing.

## 6. The practices that carry over

A draft from the gather, to confirm after his words are applied, for the inventory before launch over every category:

- **One inventory per category, generated.** The items are data naming each string's file and a literal asserted at generation; the words read from the data files where they live (core's units, the brands, the charts), never typed twice; a rendered page (the About) read from the build, so the inventory shows the words as rendered.
- **A key per string**, naming its surface and state, carried from the page to the doc, the commit, and the test that pins it.
- **Status lines**: subject first, few words, no comma (Sean, 2026-10-21); the loading, failed, and waiting lines first in every inventory, since his own testing never shows them.
- **Row words after a name**: no possessive and no plural, as the units' names (Sean, 2026-10-20), joined by a middle dot.
- **Spoken sentences** (the live region) are the visible words plus the category, so a screen reader hears what the eye sees; the inventory lists them beside the visible words, marked spoken.
- **Strings live in four places**: core's data (names, symbols, notes, facts), the converter's state (the rows' words, the lines, the headings), the components (labels, placeholders), and the About page's builder; the inventory indexes them by file.
