rows.notenote
ISO 19407’s sizes from the foot’s length, to the half size. Each brand’s row is its own chart read at that foot. Type a brand you fit (nike 10), or measure your foot heel to toe and type its length (27 cm). Measured inside, a US 9 is about the same length at every brand; the charts differ by the room each brand leaves.

The line under the table with no brand in the box: what the sizes rest on and how to anchor. packages/core/src/units/shoe-size.ts

The last sentence reads as a measurement where the room is derived (the shoe's inside less the foot the chart assigns); the precise wording is “the charts differ in the foot they give that size” (docs/TESTING.md, 2026-10-21).

rows.unit-notesnote
US men: ISO puts US men’s one size above UK; the Brannock device half a size. Youth sizes (1Y to 7Y) are men’s sizes.
US women: ISO’s scale, men’s + 1; Nike, Clarks, and KEEN count men’s + 1.5, and Zappos’s FAQ + 2.
US children: ISO’s children’s table, 4C to 13.5C; from 1Y on, youth sizes are men’s sizes.
British: A third of an inch a size, counted from a four-inch last; ISO’s shoe is two sizes longer than the foot.
British children: ISO’s children’s table, 3.5 to 13.5; the scale then restarts at 1, which is the adult UK 1.
European: Two thirds of a centimetre a size; shoes are often marked half a size above ISO (UK 4 as 37, not 36.5).
European children: ISO’s children’s table, 19.5 to 38, which gives a growing foot more room than the adult rule.
Mondopoint: The foot’s length in millimetres, marked in steps of 5 mm; China’s sizes too.
Foot length: The foot’s length in centimetres, measured heel to toe: Mondopoint in centimetres. A brand’s chart gives it where its length is the foot’s.
Japanese: A size label in centimetres, the foot’s length by ISO; brands print it about a centimetre above the foot, so its symbol is JP.

A note written in core for each scale. packages/core/src/units/shoe-size.ts

Shown nowhere in the product today: the About page lists each scale's formula and source, not its note. Show them there, or drop them.

Asics: Its NZ and AU pages say to add 1 to 1.5 cm to the foot to read its CM column, so the foot is the label less a centimetre; its US page adds a half size for running shoes.
Birkenstock: Whole EU sizes: two US half sizes share one.
Brooks: Its foot lengths are its UK site’s, brooksrunning.com/en_gb; its US page prints the same sizes without them.
Clarks: Its GB and US pages joined on the UK size they share.
Converse: The Chuck Taylor All Star table.
Crocs: Whole sizes; its EU is a pair, 43–44.
Dr. Martens: Whole sizes, the half sizes US women’s only; the brand says sizes differ between silhouettes.
New Balance: Its length column is the standard centimetre label: measured inside, its 9 is every brand’s 9, so its sizes read by ISO’s label.
On: Its FAQ says its JP label is the foot plus about a centimetre.
Puma: Its printed lengths are the shoe’s inside: at a US 9, 27.0 cm against RunRepeat’s 26.96 cm median over 18 models.
Reebok: UK and EU from its Australian guide, reebok.com.au; its US page prints US sizes and inches alone.
